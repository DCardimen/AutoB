# Solid bodies and readable combat contact

Both teams now occupy solid ground footprints. Combatants reserve open positions around their target, approach and brake into range, and hold position while attacking. Allies steer around occupied approaches. Swept relative collision prevents fast dashes and rolls from crossing through bodies between simulation steps; the mass-weighted constraint solver also separates crowded or coincident bodies in two dimensions. Heavier creatures accelerate more slowly and resist displacement more strongly. Size controls update footprints, mass and reach together.

Offensive cooldown skills wait for range, including when an execution policy is active. Support and mobility skills retain their own targeting rules. Melee damage still requires the authored weapon/appendage path to contact an opposing body; projectiles retain physical interception, misses and dodges. The native hit pose now progresses through its full 220 ms reaction instead of holding its initial sample and playing only the final 130 ms. Connected hits produce a directional impact and ground ripple; shielded contacts use a block arc. Small ground outlines show each living unit's occupied footprint.

No source atlas, palette, limb shape, equipment socket, attack contact landmark or rig geometry was edited. Existing painted-limb skinning, fixed hand grips, diagonal quadruped footfalls and charged-strike armor remain in use. Compact crab, clam and beetle footprints use 32/25/35 authored world units before display scaling; other families use at least 44. These anatomy exceptions preserve the shorter existing pincer/shell/contact paths without lengthening appendages or allowing body penetration.
